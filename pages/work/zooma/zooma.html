ZOOMA Digital Vinyl Collection

Bringing ZOOMA’s vinyl culture online: a digital record collection for everyone who shaped the company.

ZOOMA is an IT company with a musician’s heart. It started as a high-school band, and that love of music still shapes the office. At the centre of the room sits a record player surrounded by the platencollectie (the vinyl record collection). Every record stands for someone who has had an impact on the company, choses together with a short motivation of why they’ve chosen the album. The genres are as broad as the people behind them. Each workday ends with the record player spinning.

That ritual only exists inside the office. If you’re not standing next to the record player, you can’t hear it. This excludes exactly the people the collection is meant to remember: ex-colleagues, family, and future hires who should be able to taste ZOOMA’s culture before walking in. It is also a natural selling point towards investors. The decision to bring the collection online so everyone can enjoy it was obvious.

Kickoff: know the why before the what The project started with a meeting to align context, scope and the core idea. That meeting gave me the why and the who. From that point onwards I could brainstorm which features would actually serve them.

Wireframes: explore cheap, decide together. I sketched on paper first as it is the fastest way to explore how to divide the canvas and kill weak ideas before they cost anything. Then I picked the strongest concepts together with a co-worker, combined them in Figma, refined them from low-fi into high-fi, gathered one more round of feedback, and finally aligned the design with ZOOMA’s visual language: sharp edges, black, white, blue and a small touch of yellow. Minimalist, and right up my alley. However, building a brand new page on ZOOMA’s old component library was harder than expected. Every element was a decision between reusing, replacing or building from scratch.

Scope: decide what the first version must do. Together with the stakeholder, a fellow UX designer and a developer I turned the wishlist into user stories and split them across three versions. The MVP decision: a user must be able to pick an album from an overview and play it. Filters moved to version 2; streamlining text, naming and code moved to version 3.

I started implementing with the Spotify API without having researched its constraints first. That was my mistake, and it surfaced quickly. To simplify there are two Spotify API’s: one for statistics, one for playback. And the playback API requires the Spotify widget to be visible at all times.

I walked through four options:

  • Hide the widget anyway. My first honest thought was: “it doesn’t matter, I’ll just not display it. As if they’ll check”. For a personal project I might have. For a client, that is not a real option.
  • Host ZOOMA’s own server with all the music on it. Not viable for a multitude of reasons.
  • Switch to another provider, like Deezer. Their API’s were either missing functionality or behind a paywall.
  • Redesign around the constraint and be upfront with the stakeholder.

    • That last one was the call. The first version became simpler to build, and later we shipped our own spinning vinyl with Spotify controls underneath. That became the silver lining. I also made a second controller variant with just the plain Spotify controls, so the user always keeps the choice.

    Information: people before albums. What to show was its own decision. The collection is about people, so the colleague’s name and motivation come first; the album and artist name appear on hover. For filtering we debated adding “genre” and “since”, but chose only role, because that is how colleagues actually look for each other. To keep that choice reversible, I built the filter as an easily extendable part of the backend.

    Keeping it moving, and testing the dream version. The project ran party over Christmas and my exam deadlines, so I briefed colleagues to keep it going while I was away. Before launch I ran a full playtest in the office with version 3, the dream/complete version, to find bugs and check that features worked as intended. We found plenty and fixed them within two weeks.

    In the end I shipped a digital vinyl collection of everyone who works, or has worked, at ZOOMA. User can:

  • Listen to albums from the collection.
  • Browse the full platencollectie.
  • See who stands behind each record: name, role, since, motivation, plus album and artist name.
  • Filter by role to find colleagues faster.
  • Read what the collection is and why it exists.
  • Find their way back to the main ZOOMA’s website.

    • The collection became part of the workday. The thing that people loved the most is that everyone’s taste in music is vastly different, and now all of those genres are one click away.
    The stakeholders were happy. Happy enough to show it off to their families ;)

    Reflection

  • I would have made faster component decisions at the start of the design phase. The existing library was old, and I weighed reuse-versus-rebuild for too long.
  • I would research the API landscape before designing around a provider. The Spotify constraint had a major influence on the final design. Knowing the options up front would have let me design for it, not around it.
